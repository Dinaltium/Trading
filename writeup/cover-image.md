# Cover image — 16:9, PNG or JPG, required for submission

The submission gallery is a wall of thumbnails. At 200px wide a screenshot turns to mush and a
gradient looks like every other generated cover. **Typography wins at thumbnail size**, so the
image's job is to leave room for a wordmark, not to be busy.

Several teams in this track named themselves some variant of Aegis, and their covers all look
like security software — shields, locks, dark blue. Go the other way: a **terminal**, a **legal
document**, a **lab notebook**. Quiet, precise, deliberate.

The one idea to carry: **the model may decline a trade. It can never invent one.**

---

## Prompt A — the audit trail *(recommended)*

Uses the dashboard's own palette, so cover, site, deck and video end card read as one project.

> A 16:9 minimalist editorial poster. Deep espresso-black background, hex #1f1405, with a very
> subtle fine grid barely visible in the darkness. Vast empty space. Along the left third, a
> faint vertical column of dim monospace log lines suggesting an audit trail — mostly
> illegible, deliberately low contrast — with one single line picked out in warm sand #cab49b.
> A thin olive #a4905b horizontal rule sits in the lower third, spanning about a quarter of the
> width. No charts, no candlesticks, no robots, no glowing brains, no circuit-board motifs, no
> stock photography, no people, no text. Flat, print-like, high contrast, editorial restraint.
> Leave the centre-right two thirds completely empty for typography to be added afterwards.

Then set the type yourself, in the empty right two thirds:

```
BRIGHTLINE                            large · mono or a tight grotesque · #fafafa
a bright-line rule admits no          small · #a4905b · sentence case
judgment. neither does ours.
```

## Prompt B — the one-item menu

Dramatises the rulebook: one permitted choice, and the option to decline.

> A 16:9 poster photographed from directly above: a plain cream card resting on a dark walnut
> table in soft natural light. The card carries a single short line of elegant serif text and
> nothing else — enormous empty space around it. Muted, editorial, film-still quality, shallow
> depth of field, warm shadows. No people, no hands, no logos, no charts, and no text anywhere
> except that one line on the card.

## Prompt C — the pinned dial

The graveyard idea: a gauge reading maximum because it has only ever been read once.

> A 16:9 editorial poster: a single analogue gauge, needle pinned hard at maximum, isolated on
> a deep charcoal background with dramatic side lighting. Brass and glass, a scientific
> instrument, slightly worn. Minimal, moody, high contrast, enormous empty space around it. No
> text on the dial face, no charts, no digital UI, no people.

## Practical

**Generate the composition, then set the text yourself in Canva or Figma.** Image models
mangle typography — you will get `BRIGHTUNE` or `BRIGHTLIME`, and the wordmark is the first
thing a judge sees. Ask for the space to be left empty, then set the type properly.

- Export at **1920×1080**, PNG or JPG
- **Test it at 200px wide.** If the wordmark is not instantly readable, make it bigger
- The same wordmark goes on the deck title slide, the video end card, and the naming post
- Palette, matching the dashboard exactly:

| | |
|---|---|
| espresso | `#1f1405` — the ground |
| sand | `#cab49b` — the one highlighted line |
| olive | `#a4905b` — rules and the tagline |
| off-white | `#fafafa` — the wordmark |
